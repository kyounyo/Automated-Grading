
(a) Polymer-based injectable modified release systems come in a range of formats. One format is a polymer microsphere. Describe in point form the advantages and disadvantages of polymer microspheres as a dose form and what each means for the patient.  (5 marks)
(1 mark each point up to 5)
Advantages
-May be biodegradable - do not need removal
-Provides a longer duration than lipid systems, reducing frequency of administration
-Injectable system, does not require surgical implantation
-Aesthetics – no physical lump where the implant is injected
Disadvantages
•⁠  ⁠Limited to non-acid labile drugs – patient may need alternative system in this case
•⁠  ⁠Need skilled health worker to administer – may exclude remote patients
•⁠  ⁠Complex manufacture - expensive
(b) A rival technology to microspheres is ‘in situ gelling’ polymer systems. In point form briefly describe the attributes of these systems. Is there a clinical benefit to the patient for these systems over microspheres? Why do you think they are of interest?
(5 marks)
-Systems contain a solvent, or polymers that are temperature sensitive
-Generally prepared using a biodegradable polymer
-drug must be soluble with polymer in solvent
-on administration solvent diffuses away 
-drug released by degradation and erosion of the polymer matrix
-No clinical benefit, may be cheaper, 
-easier to manufacture, easier to sterilise
•⁠  ⁠Avoids needle blocking
